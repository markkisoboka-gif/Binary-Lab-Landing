System design

The Block Cycle Traversal

The Block Cycle Traversal is a proprietary trading framework combining a structured recovery mechanism with a discretionary directional signal. Every trade signal is manually generated — there is no automated or algorithmic execution involved; each call is the product of a disciplined, proprietary analytical process applied by hand, trade by trade. The system runs on a fixed sequence of thirty trades, organized into ten blocks of three trades each.

Operational flow — Each cycle begins in Block One with an initial directional call, manually generated through that same analytical process.

Correction mechanism — An incorrect call advances the system to the next trade within the current block.

Progression — Three consecutive incorrect calls within a block advance the system to the first trade of the next block.

Cycle reset — A correct call at any stage resets the cycle to Block One.

Implementation

Documented, not asserted

The system is currently deployed on a virtual trading account, managing a ten-asset portfolio — six major indices and four key commodities. Every entry across this portfolio is manually called, not automated; it's designed to generalize to any market defined by a binary win/loss outcome per trade.

Indices are traded according to each index's own standard market hours. Commodities run on a near-continuous window: the system ends trading for the day at 10:00 PM London time, when the scheduled one-hour maintenance halt begins, and resumes trading at 11:00 PM London time, once that halt ends.

System equilibrium

The design thesis

The framework is built on a specific design thesis: that within an unbreached cycle, a correct call — once it lands — offsets the accumulated cost of prior incorrect ones. This is the mechanism's intended logic, and its validity is being tested in real time against the daily published record rather than asserted as a mathematical certainty in advance.

The result is not produced by the sequence — it's produced by the method. The fixed sequence length (currently thirty trades) is a chosen containment structure, nothing more; it doesn't generate accuracy on its own. Each call within it is produced by the proprietary analytical method. The sequence's role is to measure that method's real-world performance under a defined, bounded structure — not to manufacture a result independently of it. Whether a correct call reliably lands within the current sequence length is precisely what's being tested, trade by trade — first across the closed Phase 1 record (June 29–July 18, 2026), and now across Phase 2, beginning July 19, 2026 — not assumed ahead of it.

Across Phase 1 (June 29–July 18, 2026), the sequence was not breached. Within that containment structure, a correct call was produced before any block reached its limit in every cycle recorded during that phase, and system equilibrium held throughout. That phase is now closed and stands as historical record. Phase 2 began July 19, 2026, from a fresh baseline — the ongoing log is what will show whether equilibrium holds under the current phase.
